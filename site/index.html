Listen to the Salish Sea

Orcasound's hydrophones in Haro Strait and Puget Sound stream the sound of the sea around the clock: ship engines, sea lions, and sometimes the calls of the Southern Resident and Bigg's killer whales. Pick one and listen. Hydrophones run by other groups around Vancouver Island and the Strait of Georgia are on the map below, with links to their own players.

Helping volunteers find the calls

The OrcaHello AI flags far more clips than contain orcas, and volunteers review each one. A second-stage model learns which flags people confirm, from the AI's own scores plus context such as whether other hydrophones lit up in the previous hours. Method

Triage model. Every OrcaHello detection gets the AI's confidence and segment scores, plus context:

  • How many flags the same hydrophone raised in the past hour.
  • How many the whole network raised in the past two hours.
  • How many other hydrophones raised in the past three hours, since whales move between sites.
  • Hours since this hydrophone's last flag.
  • Time of day and season.

A gradient-boosted classifier learns the human verdict on 2020–2024 flags and is scored on 2025–2026.

Outlook. Weekly logistic regression on season, last week's whale reports and the share of the last four weeks with reports, at each hydrophone. Reports come from the Orcasound live-listening app. It's validated on 2025–2026 by Brier score against a hydrophone-and-month climatology.

Ships. The latest 14 days of US Coast Guard AIS that NOAA/BOEM Marine Cadastre has published, clipped to 47–51° N, 122–128.6° W. Moving vessels (over 0.8 knots) are split into tracks at gaps over 30 minutes, thinned to one-minute fixes, simplified to about 40 m and grouped into seven AIS ship types. Each day is its own file, loaded only when you pick it. A live snapshot from aisstream.io is added when the free AISSTREAM_API_KEY secret is set.

Sightings. Acartia's public feed only serves the last week, so every daily run is merged into an archive that grows from 6 October 2026. iNaturalist adds the last four months of cetacean photos (research grade and needs-ID). Listener reports are people pressing "whale" in the Orcasound app, counted per hydrophone and day.

Whale-sound tier list. An editorial score out of 100, not a trained model. It covers the last 30 days of OrcaHello clips, grouped into encounters (same hydrophone, gaps under 30 minutes, best clip shown), and Orcasound listener events (reports at one hydrophone less than 20 minutes apart). Clips a moderator rejected are left out.

  • Verification, 30. Moderator confirmed 30, moderator unsure 12, listener reports 10, AI only 5.
  • AI confidence, 20. OrcaHello's confidence. A listener event borrows it from an AI flag at the same hydrophone within ±10 minutes, else 0.
  • Species, 20. Southern Resident 20 (moderator tags such as SRKW, J/K/L pod or S-call types), other orca 14, humpback 9, unknown 4. An AI label alone scores a little less (resident 15, Bigg's 11, humpback 7). A sighting within 25 km and an hour can fill in an unknown species.
  • Clarity, 15. Number of positive AI segments (up to 6) and the strongest segment's score. For listener events, the number of reports (up to 6).
  • Corroboration, 10. Listener reports or AI flags at the same hydrophone within ±1 hour, another hydrophone active within ±1 hour, or a sighting within 25 km and ±1 hour.
  • Recency, 5. Halves roughly every five days.

Tiers are fixed cut-offs: S 85+, A 70+, B 55+, C 40+, D below. The weights are a judgement about what makes a clip worth hearing. Change them and the order changes, so read the tiers as a guide, not a measurement.

Honest limitations

  • Reports depend on hydrophones being online and people listening. Quiet weeks can mean no whales, a microphone that was down, or nobody tuned in. Only weeks when a hydrophone had some listener activity are modelled.
  • Volunteers sometimes disagree, and the mix of hydrophones and AI model versions changed over time. The 2025–2026 test reflects today's system, which is the honest test.
  • Ships in Canadian waters are under-counted. NOAA's AIS comes from US Coast Guard shore receivers. They hear the southern Strait of Georgia and Juan de Fuca well, but reception fades north of Nanaimo, and Johnstone Strait and the west coast of Vancouver Island are essentially outside it. An empty stretch of water there means no data, not no ships. Canadian Coast Guard AIS isn't openly published, and the panel beside the map shows vessels per day by region.
  • Ship dates don't match whale dates. NOAA publishes AIS in batches months after the fact (the latest is the end of June 2026). Ships for today need the aisstream.io key. Without it the page says so and shows no ships, never made-up ones.
  • Hydrophones outside Orcasound can't be played here. Ocean Networks Canada needs a free account and token. SIMRES, Raincoast and OrcaLab stream on their own sites. Some positions are approximate.
  • Sighting history is short. Acartia history on this page only goes back to 6 October 2026, because the public feed holds a week and the archive started then. Whale reports are opportunistic: more near Seattle, Victoria and the San Juans partly reflects where people are watching.
  • The outlook counts every whale report in the Orcasound app. That includes AI detections OrcaHello posts there, not only people. The map and tier list use people's reports only.
